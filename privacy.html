Privacy

Last updated: 27 November 2026

TrailSafe is a community trail safety app. Knowing where fellow trail users are makes the trail safer for everyone. This page says what we keep, who can see it, and how you stay in charge of it.

What we keep about you

  • Your email address, your first name and surname, and whether you are a runner, hiker or cyclist. When you ask to join a community, also your mobile number (checked with a code we send by SMS), and anything you choose to add to help members of your community recognise you (for example your Strava profile link or your club).
  • What members see while you wait to join. Members of the community you asked to join see your name, whether you run, hike or cycle, the last 5 digits of your mobile number, the first two letters and last letter of your email address with its ending (for example da•••••••h@gmail.com), and anything you added to help them recognise you. This lets people who know you recognise you and vouch for you. Your community’s admins and TrailSafe see your full number and email address, and your admin may contact you to check it’s really you. Once you have joined, or if you withdraw your request, members no longer see these details. We never share your number with anyone else, and never use it for anything else.
  • Vouches and concerns. When you vouch for someone, or raise a concern about someone asking to join, we keep your name with it. Your community’s admins and TrailSafe can see who vouched, and the person you vouch for sees your first name and initial. Only TrailSafe sees concerns, and the person asking is never told who raised one.
  • If you ask us to add your trail group, we keep what you tell us about the group, your mobile number, and, if you give them, the name and mobile number of the group’s admin, so we can check the group before we add it.
  • Which community you belong to, and a record that you accepted how TrailSafe works.
  • Your choice about showing or hiding your location when you head out.
  • Your phone’s notification address, so TrailSafe can send you alerts.
  • A running total of the time you have spent out on the trails, used only to work out your free coffee. Only the TrailSafe back-end team can see it. We also keep the 5-digit code you ask for when you redeem a coffee, until it has been used or has expired.
  • Anything you send us yourself, such as a bug report, a feature request, a coffee shop suggestion or a request to move to another community. If you attach a screenshot, we keep that too. Only the TrailSafe owner and developer team see these, and only to improve the app.
  • If something goes wrong inside the app, the app sends us a short report so we can fix it: what failed, where in the app, the app version, and your phone’s model and system version. It never includes your name, email, account or position. Only the TrailSafe developer team sees these reports.
  • How the app is used. Which screens you open, how often and for how long you use TrailSafe, and your phone’s model, system version and app version. Google Analytics for Firebase collects this for us. It identifies a phone by a random number that is not linked to your name, email address or account. Google works out your country and city from your internet connection, never from your position on the trails, and we never send it your position. Google keeps this for up to 14 months.
  • Messages from TrailSafe. When we send you a message in the app, we note whether you opened it, tapped its notification or its button, and whether notifications are switched on for TrailSafe on your phone. We only ever look at these as totals, never at what one person did, and messages are deleted after 90 days.
  • If the app crashes. Google Firebase Crashlytics sends us a crash report: what failed, where in the app, the app version, your phone’s model and system version, and a random installation number. It never includes your name, email, account or position.

We keep your email address, name and mobile number for as long as you are on TrailSafe.

Your location

TrailSafe uses your location only while you are out, after you tap Start. We keep your latest position only, not a list of where you have been. When you stop, it is removed. We keep no location history. Your free coffee uses your position as described below, and keeps only a total of time.

Visible only to approved community members, and only while you’re out.

You can choose to hide your location. Members still know you’re out nearby, but can’t see where you are. Hidden locations are never displayed with a name or any other identifying detail. On the Dashboard map they appear only as anonymous points.

Other communities. Approved members of other TrailSafe communities within 20 km of you can see you as a dot on the map while you are out. They never see your name. Your own community sees your name, unless you have hidden your location.

Looking from home. When you open the Dashboard to see how many community members are out near you, TrailSafe uses a single position from your phone to find the right area. We do not store it.

Your free coffee

While you are out, our server works out how much time you spend moving along the trails, to fill your coffee mug. It uses the position your phone already sends us while you are out, and it does this even when you have hidden your location from other members.

A moment counts only when you are inside a trail area marked on the TrailSafe map and moving at walking-to-cycling speed (about 2 to 25 km/h). At most 6 hours a day count. We do not keep where you were or when: all we keep is a running total of time. Only the TrailSafe back-end team can see it.

When your total reaches 30 hours you can ask for a code. The code is 5 digits, works once and is valid for 7 days. You show it to the coffee shop, whose staff type it into a TrailSafe page. When the code is accepted, the coffee shop sees your first name and the first letter of your last name, and the sponsor’s name. We also keep an anonymous count of the coffees each community’s shop has served, for 2 months, with no name or other detail about you.

Trail reminders (optional)

Trail reminders are off unless you turn them on in Your profile.

If you turn them on, your phone needs location "Allow all the time" (Android) or "Always" (iPhone). Your phone itself notices when you reach one of your trail areas and reminds you to press Start. This happens on your phone. It does not send your location to TrailSafe, and TrailSafe is not told when a reminder was shown. The only thing your phone asks us for is the list of trail areas for your region, which is not personal.

You can turn reminders off at any time in Your profile, or take away the permission in your phone’s settings.

Alerts

When you send an alert, members within 5 km are told, and see where you are. People in your own community also see your name. Members of other communities see only that a member of your community has issued an alert. It also appears in your community’s alert log for 7 days, including who sent it, where it started, when, and how it ended. Each entry is deleted 7 days after it ends.

TrailSafe is not an emergency service. In an emergency, always call 112 as well.

Who helps us run TrailSafe

We use these companies to run the app. Each one only handles what it needs to do its job.

  • Supabase: our database and sign-in, hosted in Ireland.
  • Resend: sends you your email verification code.
  • Twilio: sends you your mobile verification code by SMS. It receives your mobile number and the code, nothing else.
  • Google Firebase: sends notifications to Android phones, and provides the usage statistics (Google Analytics) and crash reports (Crashlytics) described above. We have switched off advertising features, and we have not allowed Google to use this data for its own purposes.
  • Apple: sends notifications to iPhones. (applies once the iPhone app is released)
  • Cloudflare: a short check that you are a person, when you sign in or sign up.
  • Google: if you choose to sign in with Google, Google tells us your name and email address and that it is really you. We receive nothing else from your Google account, and Google learns that you signed in to TrailSafe.

Our maps are served from our own files, so no map company receives your location. We do not sell your information and we do not show advertising.

Your choices

  • Show or hide your location each time you start, or ask the app to ask you every time.
  • Turn trail reminders on or off.
  • Stop sharing at any time by tapping Stop. Your position is removed.
  • Delete your account in the app: open the menu, then Your profile, then Delete my account. Everything we hold about you is deleted straight away, and it can’t be undone. Two things stay: an alert you sent, whose entry in your community’s alert log remains until 7 days after it ended, without your name; and anonymous usage statistics and crash reports, which are not linked to your account and contain no name, email or position.
  • Usage statistics and crash reports are part of how the app works and can’t be switched off inside it. They carry no name, email or position.
